Metro Railways General Rules, 2020

CHAPTER II: Rules Applying to Authorised Persons Generally

Subject: Official code of conduct, duties, competencies, and safety protocols for all authorised metro personnel.

3 & 4. Supply and Upkeep of Rules

Rules 3 & 4

Rule 3: Supply of Copies

The metro railway administration shall supply a copy of these rules and amendments made therein to:

  • Operation control centre.
  • Each station.
  • Each rake maintenance depot, traction depot, permanent way depot and signal depot.
  • Such other offices as may be specified.
  • Each authorised person on whom responsibility has been placed under these rules.

Rule 4: Upkeep of the Copy

Every authorised person who has been supplied with a copy of these rules shall:

  • Keep it posted with all corrections.
  • Produce the same on demand by any of his superiors.
  • Obtain a new copy from his superior in case his copy is lost or defaced.
  • Ensure that the staff working under him are supplied with all corrections or amendments and that they comply with the provisions.

5. Knowledge of Rules and Competency

Rule 5
Knowledge
Rule 5(1)		 Every authorised person shall be fully conversant with the rules relating to his duties; pass the examinations as specified by the authorised officer; satisfy himself that the staff working under him are conversant with the rules relating to their duties and obtain a written assurance from them.
Competency
Rule 5(2)		 No authorised person shall be assigned any duty under these rules unless he has passed the prescribed examination as regards his technical ability and skills and knowledge of rules, regulations, procedures and manuals relevant to his duties and has been issued, after being found fit, a certificate of competency.

6, 7 & 9. Assistance, Obedience & Attendance

Rules 6, 7 & 9

6. Assistance in observance

Every authorised person shall render assistance in carrying out these rules and report promptly any breach thereof, which may come to his notice, to his superior officer and other authority concerned.

7. Obedience to rules and orders

Every authorised person shall observe and obey all rules and special instructions; and all lawful orders given by his superior officials.

9. Attendance to duty

Every authorised person shall be on duty at such times and places and for such period as may be fixed by the metro railway administration and shall also attend at any other time and place where his services may be required.

8. Prevention of Trespass, Damage or Loss

Rule 8

Security and Protection

Every authorised person shall be responsible for the security and protection of the property of metro railway under his charge or possession.

Endeavour to Prevent

Every authorised person shall endeavour to prevent:

  • Trespass on metro railway premises;
  • Theft, damage or loss of metro railway property;
  • Injury to passengers, others and himself; and
  • Fire and other unsafe incidents in metro railway premises.

10 & 11. Absence & Intoxicants

Rules 10 & 11

10. Absence from duty

  • No authorised person shall, without prior permission of his superior officer, absent himself from duty or alter his appointed hours of attendance or exchange duty with any other authorised person or leave his charges of duty unless properly relieved.
  • In case any authorised person, while on duty, desires to absent himself from duty on the ground of illness, he shall immediately report the matter to his superior officer and shall not leave his duty unless an authorised person has been placed in charge thereof.

11. Taking alcoholic drink, sedative, narcotic, stimulant drug

  • An authorised person shall not take or use any alcoholic drink, sedative, narcotic or stimulant drug or preparation within eight hours before the commencement of his duty or take or use any such drink, drug or preparation while on duty.
  • No authorised person, while on duty, shall be in a state of intoxication or in a state in which, by reason of his having taken or used any alcoholic drink, sedative, narcotic or stimulant drug or preparation, his capacity to perform his duties is impaired.
  • An authorised person while on duty, shall not smoke or chew tobacco.

12. Conduct of Authorised Persons

Rule 12

Every authorised person shall:

  • A
    Wear the uniform and badge as prescribed by the metro railway administration and be neat and tidy in his appearance while on duty.
  • B
    Be prompt, civil and courteous.
  • C
    Not solicit or accept illegal gratification.
  • D
    Give all reasonable assistance and be careful to give correct information to the public.
  • E
    Make complete and truthful statement at all times in all reports pertaining to his duty.
  • F
    When asked, give his name and designation without hesitation.

13. Duty for Ensuring Safety

Rule 13 (1) & (2)
General Safety
Rule 13(1)		 Every authorised person shall see that every effort is made for ensuring safety of the public and of his fellow person; promptly, report to his superior any occurrence likely to affect the safe and proper working of the metro railway; and render spontaneously all possible assistance when called upon to do so by the appropriate official in case of an accident or obstruction.

Immediate Action Required

Every authorised person who observes the following shall take immediate steps to prevent the accident and promptly report the matter to the OCC or the nearest Station Controller:

  • Anything wrong with a train.
  • Any obstruction, failure or threatened failure of any part of the way or works or overhead electric equipment or third rail electric equipment.
  • Any defective signal.
  • Any unusual circumstances such as fire, smoke, flood accident or other dangerous condition on any part of the system likely to interfere with the safe running of trains, or the safety of the public.

14 & 15. Standard Time & Access Control

Rules 14 & 15

14. Standard Time

The working of trains between stations on metro railway shall be regulated by the Indian Standard Time.

15. Access Control

All authorised persons, in addition to identity cards, shall be provided with access control cards or authorisation signed by their controlling officers to allow them to visit places of metro railway system with restricted access in line of their duties. The following places in particular shall have restricted access:

  • Guideways, viaducts and tunnels.
  • Receiving sub-station.
  • Traction substation.
  • Auxiliary substation.
  • Signalling equipment room.
  • Telecommunication equipment room.
  • Uninterrupted power supply room.
  • Station control room.
  • Operation control centre.
  • Back-up control centre, where provided.
  • Depot control centre.
  • Demarcated UTO territory in Depot.

CHAPTER III: Signals and Control

Subject: Comprehensive guidelines on types of signals, route setting, communications, and handling system failures.

16. General Signalling

Rule 16

Types of Signals

The following signals shall be used for controlling the movements of trains on the metro railway, namely:

  • (a) Cab signals;
  • (b) Fixed signals and equipments;
  • (c) Hand signals;
  • (d) Virtual signals.

Placement & Visibility

A fixed signal shall be placed where practicable, on the left hand side of the track to which it refers, unless authorised otherwise under special instructions. It shall be visible from such a distance as shall enable a Train Operator to brake a train from twenty-five kilometer per hour speed to stop before reaching the fixed signal.

17. Description of Signals: Cab Signals

Rule 17 (1)

Train movements on running tracks and in depot (if equipped with ATP) shall normally be governed by the automatic train protection system which displays to the Train Operator in the operating console:

Console Displays

  • (a) Actual speed of the train;
  • (b) The maximum permitted speed at each point of travel;
  • (c) The distance the train is currently authorised to travel (where provided);
  • (d) System alarms; and
  • (e) Messages.

Target Indications

In case the target speed indication and the target distance indication, where provided, are greater than zero, the indication is referred to as "PROCEED" indication.

In case either of these indications is "0", the indication is referred to as "STOP" indication.

17. Description of Signals: Fixed Signals

Rule 17 (2) & (3)
Red Aspect Indicates that a train must be brought to Stop short of the signal when driven in RM or ROS or cut out mode.
White/Violet Aspect Indicates that the route is set and locked but may not be fully clear upto the next fixed signal. A train operating under cab signals may proceed under the authority of the cab signals but a train operating on the sole authority of line side signals must stop and seek instructions from the Traffic Controller.
Green Aspect Indicates that the route is cleared to the next fixed signal and the train may proceed as far as the next fixed signal.
Not in Use: When a fixed signal is not in use, the aspect shall be covered and the cover shall display two crossed white bars on a black background, the bars being not less than thirty centimetres long and ten centimetres wide.

17. Hand Signals & Virtual Signals

Rule 17 (4) & (5)

Hand Signals for Shunting & Movement

Hand signals shall normally be used only for the shunting of work train in depot or at the site of work or in extreme emergency.

Aspect Indication
A green lamp or green flag moved slowly up and down Move away from the signal
A green lamp or green flag waved from side to side across the body Move towards the signal
Display red light or red flag Stop

Virtual Signal

These are not physical signals but locations on line. These are used as an intermediate entry or exit location... displayed on interlocking and ATS monitors.

18 & 19. Provision and Working of Signals

Rules 18 & 19

18. Provision of Signals

  • Fixed signals shall be provided on running lines at the approach to all points and crossings of interlocked areas.
  • All depot tracks and any other tracks not equipped with ATP shall be controlled by fixed signals for both entry to and exit from the main line.
  • Stopping markers shall be provided for all platforms to indicate where a train of a given length shall stop.

19. Working of Signals and Points

  • Control of signals and points shall be from a route setting panel or work-station, and complete routes, points and signals, shall be cleared by a single set of operations under normal conditions.
  • Any route which has been cleared for a train shall not be cancelled until it is cleared by the train entering the route except in case of emergency or alternative route clearance.

20. Control of Signalling & Safety Communication

Rule 20 (1) & (2)

Main Line Control

Main line signalling is controlled from the OCC and operates normally under automatic control with routes being set and train intervals regulated by computer control. In case of complete failure of control from the OCC, the entire control may be transferred to a standby backup control centre, if provided at an alternate location.

Safety Communication Protocol

  • All communication between the OCC and Train Operators, Station Controller... shall be recorded with time stamping, and preserved for incident analysis and training.
  • Messages affecting train movements shall be addressed by the Traffic Controller to only one train at a time.
  • In an emergency, a blanket message may be sent by the Traffic Controller to all trains in or approaching a particular area, which must be acknowledged individually.

20. Running Lines & Depot Tracks

Rule 20 (3) & (5)

Running Lines

The signalling system on the running line shall be an ATP System.

  • In case the speed which permits a train running on the ATP to stop under normal braking within a limit of safety is exceeded... an irrevocable emergency brake shall automatically apply.
  • Where no cab signal is available... the Train Operator shall proceed under the ATP not exceeding the speed shown on the TCMS or TIMS.

Depot Tracks

The depot tracks may not be equipped with full complement of ATP and trains shall be automatically restricted to a maximum speed of twenty five kilometres per hour.

  • Movements within depots shall be controlled by fixed colour light signals or position light type signals.
  • Control of movements within the depot shall be exercised by a depot controller from a control panel or work-station.

21. Train Detained on Line

Rule 21
Detention Trigger In case a train operating under the ATO or cab signals comes to a stop on a running line and does not receive a proceed code within sixty seconds, the Train Operator shall inform the Traffic Controller by the radio and ask for instructions.
Traffic Controller Action The Traffic Controller shall check his indications and if he is satisfied that there is no train ahead, he may instruct the Train Operator to proceed in ROS Mode or RM Mode, as the case may be, exercising utmost vigilance so that he may stop short of any obstruction.
Upgrade/Resumption In case, after travelling some distance, a proceed indication is received by the train, the train shall automatically upgrade to the Coded Manual Mode of control and the Train Operator shall again inform the Traffic Controller.
Manual Points Operation: In case points are not indicated as set and locked, the Traffic Controller shall instruct the Station Controller to examine the concerned points. If they are lying in the incorrect position, he shall manually set the points from the station control room with the crank handle interlocked through a key... and the points shall be secured with a clamp and padlocked.

22. Absence of Cab Signalling

Rule 22

Emergency Brake

Failure of the ATP shall initiate an irrevocable emergency brake application. The Train Operator shall report the occurrence to the Traffic Controller and seek instructions.

Proceeding under RM/ROS Mode

The Traffic Controller shall verify... that the problem is not caused by a track side fault or another train and if no cause is apparent, he shall instruct the Train Operator to select the RM Mode or ROS Mode and try to move ahead exercising utmost vigilance.

Cut Out Mode Authorisation

In case the train still does not move, the Traffic Controller shall authorise the Train Operator to operate the cut out switch and to proceed in cut out mode of control upto a suitable station, so as to detrain the passengers and withdraw the train as per special instructions. During the cut out mode of control, the Train Operator shall exercise utmost vigilance so that he is able to stop short of any obstruction.

23 & 24. Failure of Fixed Signals and Routes

Rules 23 & 24

23. Failure of Fixed Signals

In case a Train operator observes that a fixed signal is displaying:

  • a) No aspect (is blank); or
  • b) Multiple aspects; or
  • c) Flickering of aspects,

Then the signal should be treated as ON (Red) in case train is not running under the authority of cab signals and Train Operator shall stop his train and seek instructions from Traffic Controller by radio.

24. Failure of Route Setting

  • In case a route through an interlocked area cannot be set automatically or by manual control from the OCC, the control of the area shall be transferred to the local station control room.
  • In case the route cannot be set, the Traffic Controller shall instruct the Train Operator to secure his train and wait for instructions.

CHAPTER IV: Speed and Working of Trains

Subject: Comprehensive rules on operator competency, train speeds, defect handling, and locomotive working.

25. General Requirements & Competency

Rule 25 (1-3)

No person shall drive a train unless he is in possession of a valid certificate of competency and medical fitness. No Train Operator shall be booked until he has learnt the road (minimum 3 round trips, including 1 night trip).

25. Driving Direction & Stabling

Rule 25 (4-9)

Cab Operation

  • No train shall be driven on a running line from the rear cab except under an engineer's possession or exceptional circumstances (max 10 kmph with lookout).
  • In the depot, the train shall always be driven from the leading cab in the direction of travel.
  • If a damaged train cannot be driven from the leading end, a lookout shall be posted at the leading end with means of audible warning and emergency brake.

Train Lighting

  • Manned Train: Two white lights to the front and two red lights to the rear in the direction of travel.
  • Stationary (Running Line): Shall be secured and show two red lights at each end.
  • Stabled (Depot/Siding): Shall show at least one red light at each end at a double ended siding and at the outermost end in a dead end siding.

26. Service Regularity

Rule 26

Every effort shall be made by OCC Staff, station staff and train staff to ensure that scheduled intervals between trains are maintained.

Traffic Controller (TC)

Responsible for maintaining services and restoring them following a delay. The TC may adjust the timetable and intervene manually to set and clear routes.

Terminal Stations

Each Train Operator shall start his train from terminal station as soon as he gets the departure order indication.

Intermediate Stations

The Train Operator shall start from intermediate stations at the time indicated by the departure order and follow any instruction from the TC varying scheduled timings.

27. Speed of Trains

Rule 27 (1-5)
Operating Mode
Speed Regulation & Restriction
Automatic Mode (AM)
Automatically controlled by the CATC system.
Coded Manual Mode
Regulated according to cab signals. Exceeding safety limits results in irrevocable emergency brakes.
RM or ROS Mode
Automatically regulated to 25 km/h. Operator shall further restrict as required.
Cut Out Mode
Manually regulated. Power cuts off above 25 km/h (low speed cut out) or 40 km/h (high speed cut out).

27 & 28. Platform Speeds & Caution Orders

Rule 27 (6-8) & Rule 28

Platform & Shunting Speeds

  • If it is not possible to close any platform screen door or edge door, the speed of the train shall be restricted to 20 km/h and an audible warning sounded.
  • Maximum speed in shunting shall not exceed limits prescribed in special instructions.
  • Speed in poor visibility shall be governed by special instructions.

28. Caution Order

Whenever track or traction power is under repair, a caution order detailing kilometres, reason, and speed limits shall be handed to the Train Operator at the stopping station short of the place.

The Traffic Controller and Station Controller shall ensure the caution order is suitably incorporated in the CATC system.

29. Train Staffing & Pre-Departure

Rule 29 (1-6)
  • 1
    Manning: Each train shall be manned by only one Train Operator.
  • 2
    Cab Access: No person shall travel in the cab except an authorised trainee, driving inspector, or specified personnel.
  • 3
    Equipments: Operator must possess a handbook, two pairs of required spectacles, a tri-colour torch, and a first aid kit.
  • 4
    Notices: Operator must examine any notice issued for his guidance before reporting for duty.
  • 5
    Testing: The Train Operator shall undergo a breathalyser test at sign-on and sign-off.
  • 6
    Brake Test: Operator shall check continuity/adequacy of brake power and conduct a brake power feel test at the first available opportunity.

29. Incapacitated Train Operator

Rule 29 (7)
If a Train Operator becomes incapacitated while driving, he shall, if capable, inform the Traffic Controller who shall inform the Station Controller at the next station.

If Train is under ATO

It shall arrive at the next station under automatic control. The Station Controller, having a valid competency certificate, may work as Train Operator until another is made available.

If Train is under Manual Control

If stalled between stations, the rear Station Controller boards the following train and proceeds in RM/Cut out mode to the rear of the stalled train. He then boards the stalled train, drives it to the next station, and continues until relieved.

30. Train Defects (Mechanical & Traction)

Rule 30 (1-6)

Safety Equipment & Traction

  • No train with defective safety equipments, cab signalling, brakes or doors shall remain in passenger service.
  • If traction power is lost on one bogie, train continues. If on more than one bogie causing delay, passengers detrained and train worked empty.
  • Mechanical defect causing locked axle: train stopped immediately and not moved until cleared by rolling stock supervisor.

Cab Driving Failure

If train cannot be driven/braked from leading cab, passengers detrained. Station Controller acts as lookout in leading cab while Operator drives from rear cab in Cut Out mode (max 10 km/h, comms via cab telephone).

30. Train Defects (Systems & Brakes)

Rule 30 (7-9)

TCMS / TIMS Failure

Train Operator shall bring train to a complete stop. If train can be operated using suitable isolations, consult Traffic Controller. If still inoperative, train is stalled and requires assistance/rescue.

Brake Failure

Failure to apply/release indicated on TCMS/TIMS. If one bogie fails to apply, train works per special instructions. If failure affects designed brake effort, train withdrawn at end of trip. In no case shall the train be moved without release of all brakes.

30. Train Defects (Doors & Utilities)

Rule 30 (10-12)
  • A
    Doors: Train shall not start if doors are not closed. If some doors do not open but close successfully, train remains in service with announcements. In peak periods, it may be withdrawn to avoid delays.
  • B
    Air Conditioning: Failure indicated on TCMS/TIMS. Has no effect on safety, but must be reported to OCC for prompt attention.
  • C
    Lighting: If main lighting fails on 1 or 2 cars, train continues with emergency lighting until terminal. If both main and emergency fail on the same car, passengers detrained at next station and rake withdrawn.

31. Examination of Trains

Rule 31

Each train shall be examined by a competent person before passenger service to ensure all functions and safety devices work correctly.

  • Cab signalling & Safety brake circuits.
  • Train radio & Display panel of TCMS/TIMS.
  • Head and tail lights & Brake gears.
  • Isolating cock ties, MCB & safety switch seals intact.

Competent staff signs a certificate of safety test. Operator checks this certificate's validity and tests head/tail lights, marker lights, and speedometer before moving from stabling.

32. Duties of a Train Operator (Driving)

Rule 32 (1-8)
Vigilance Shall pay immediate attention and obey every signal, remaining vigilant and cautious at all times.
Defective Signal Cannot pass a fixed signal at 'on' or 'defective' without written authority or radio authorisation.
Track & Doors Adjust speed for slippery tracks (grease, water). Observe platform end while closing doors to avoid striking passengers.
AM Operation In AM, start train after closing doors; doors open automatically at next station. In manual, doors not opened until complete stop.

32. Duties of a Train Operator (Stations)

Rule 32 (9-14)

Stopping Accuracy

  • Short Stop: Manually drive to proper stopping place before opening doors.
  • Overshoot (On Platform): Contact Traffic Controller for permission to reverse.
  • Overshoot (Beyond Platform): Seek instructions. Reverse if possible; else proceed to next station without opening doors, or use exterior emergency door controls.

Emergencies & Visibility

  • Passenger Alarm: Establish voice comms. Unless clear/immediate danger, proceed to next station. Can reset with TC approval if no response.
  • Poor Visibility: Advise Traffic Controller and proceed per special instructions.
  • Manual Drive: Ensure doors open on the platform side only.

33. Locomotives & Work Trains

Rule 33
Operation: ATP equipped locos/vehicles worked as per passenger train rules. Operator must hold competency certificate (or be accompanied by one).
Pre-Departure: Operator must ensure train is fully coupled, conduct pneumatic brake continuity test, and verify handbrakes released.
Non-ATP Vehicles: Taken on running line only if coupled to equipped vehicle, within engineer's possession, or running on line of sight method during non-revenue period.
Securing & Restrictions: Unpowered vehicles secured by handbrakes. Red lamp at outermost ends. Fly shunting of any vehicle is expressly prohibited at any time.

34. Kinds of Platform Doors

Rule 34
  • 1
    Platform Screen Doors (PSD): Powered doors located along the platform edge throughout the passenger area. Door locations strictly correspond to the train car passenger door locations.
  • 2
    Platform Edge Doors (PED): Powered doors located along the platform at the platform edge.
  • 3
    Manual Secondary Doors (MSD): Manual doors located at one end or both ends of the platform. Provide access between the platform and the trackside for staff.

34. Kinds of Platform Doors (Cont.)

Rule 34

Emergency Escape Doors (EED)

Located around Platform Screen Doors of leading and trailing passenger cars. Meant strictly for use in emergency situations.

Fixed Panel

Platform length sections not provided with PSD, PED, EED, or MSD. Fitted with fixed screens to prevent track access.

35. Normal working of doors (PSD)

Rule 35 (1)
Signalling Link Opening/closing shall be after receipt of the DOORS OPEN or DOORS CLOSE command signals from the signalling link.
Accuracy Automatic operation is enabled ONLY when the train stops within ±500mm (or defined limit) of its normal stopping position.
Repositioning If a train stops outside this limit, it may be driven under Train Operator’s control in Cut out mode to reposition it. This allows normal operation of PSDs when correctly positioned.

35. Normal working of doors (PED & MSD)

Rule 35 (2 & 3)

(2) Platform Edge Door

Opening or closing of the PED, where provided, will be by an authorised employee, after receipt of indication of the DOORS OPEN or DOORS CLOSE command signals.

(3) Manual Secondary Door

The Manual Secondary Door may be opened from the platform side by using a special maintenance staff key.

36. Abnormal working (PSD)

Rule 36 (1)
  • A
    Passenger Action: If PSD doesn't open automatically, passengers can go to the platform by opening the PSD using the manual release handle located on the track side of the door.
  • B
    Staff Action: If PSD fails to open/close automatically, the Train Operator or maintenance staff may open/close all PSDs using a local control panel, or individual PSDs using a staff special key.
  • C
    Isolation: Any defective PSD may be isolated by using staff special key.

20 KMPH

36. Abnormal working (PED)

Rule 36 (2)
Signal Fail If local panel doesn't receive DOOR OPEN/CLOSE signals, staff can provide indication using PSD local panel with staff special key.
Isolation Authorized employees can open/close individual PEDs and isolate defective PEDs using special staff key.
Restriction Max speed 20 km/h for unclosed PEDs. An audible warning shall be sounded by all trains while entering the station.

36. Emergency Evacuation (MSD & EED)

Rule 36

Manual Secondary Door (MSD)

Opened from trackside using a push bar. Designed to swing open and be held at 90°. Automatically reverts to closed/locked safely if left open at < 90° without staff intervention.

Emergency Escape Door (EED)

Used if train stops incorrectly. Passengers detrain by pressing emergency push bar on track side. Same 90° hold/auto-close physics as MSD.

Note: Any door manually released from track/platform side shall then be subjected to a gentle reclosing force.

37. Indications (Door Open & Secondary)

Rule 37 (1 & 2)

Fully Opened Position Illuminated (Solid) Deactivated

MSD / EED Indicators

One Light Emitting Diode (LED) or suitable indicator is provided near each MSD or EED leaf to give closed and locked indications.

37. Indications (Obstruction Detection)

Rule 37 (3)

Trapped Person Sensors (Train & PSD gap)

  • Nobody trapped = Green
  • Somebody trapped = Red Flashing. System will NOT give clearance for train departure.

Obstruction Between PSD Leaves

  • Door attempts to close automatically again.
  • If obstacle removed → PSD closes.
  • If obstacle persists → PSD stops in unlocked position (can be pushed back by hand).
  • Once cleared, Driver operates doors again. If persistent, Station Staff must attend.

CHAPTER VI: Control and Working of Stations

Subject: Regulations on station classes, roles of station personnel, station security, crowd management, and emergency evacuation procedures.

38 & 39. Station Class & Controller Duties

Rules 38 & 39

38. Class of Stations

The stations in metro railway are classified as under:

  • Terminal stations.
  • Inter-locked stations having points and crossings, fixed signals and siding.
  • Other intermediate stations not having points and crossings (with or without fixed signals).
  • Any other class as specified in the special instructions.

39. Responsibilities of Station Controller

  • Open the station at least ten minutes before the scheduled time of the first train.
  • Carry out inspection of the station premises at the start of his duty period recording any defects or irregularities.
  • Be responsible for the supervision of the passenger flow, escalators, and staff assistance.
  • Observe the departure of the last train; inspect and lock all entrances.
  • Keep a log book detailing occurrences, inspections, complaints, and instructions.

40 & 41. Platform & Baggage Supervisors

Rules 40 & 41

40. Platform Supervisor

Where provided, shall monitor boarding and alighting; operate the emergency stop plunger if a dangerous situation arises; handle malfunctioning Platform Screen Doors; manage customer interface, platform attendance, trolley circulation, and be responsible for cleanliness of the platform.

41. Baggage Handling Supervisor

Wherever the baggage handling system is provided, the baggage handling supervisor shall monitor the entire baggage handling system to ensure smooth movement of passenger baggage; and take appropriate action as provided in special instructions in case of any abnormality.

42. Responsibilities of Booking Office Staff

Rule 42
Area of Duty
Specific Responsibility
Sales & Cash
Sell tickets for prices in the current fare table, render exact change, account for all tickets/cash, and keep surplus in locked safe or secure storage.
Vending Machines
Ensure self-service machines are adequately stocked with tickets and cash is regularly removed to secure storage.
Emergencies & Faults
Report malfunctioning equipment to maintenance and assist passengers during crowd control and emergent evacuation.
Senior Staff/Assistant Station Controller: Shall be responsible for the accurate accounting of the tickets sold and cash received; and assist or depute for the Station Controller, when circumstances demand.

43. Security of Stations

Rule 43

Public Access & Emergency Exits

  • Stations open to public at least 10 minutes before first train and until all passengers leave after the last train; otherwise secured against unauthorised entry.
  • Emergency exit key provided in a glass box near control room for maintenance staff during non-traffic hours. Checked daily by Station Controller.
  • Opening of emergency exit doors shall be monitored by suitable mechanism; may also be used for passenger evacuation.

Secured Areas & Valuables

  • All equipment rooms kept locked at all times when access is not required; authorised persons must prevent unauthorised access.
  • All areas not required for the passage of passengers shall be secured.
  • Booking offices and places storing items of value (tickets/cash) locked at all times with items kept in safe storage.

44. Station Working Orders

Rule 44

Each station shall be provided with the station working orders applicable to the station, giving details of:

  • (a) The location of equipment and guidelines for their use.
  • (b) The emergency evacuation routes at station with adjoining block section.
  • (c) The designated entrance for attendance by police, fire and ambulance vehicle.
  • (d) The designated entry for fire services.
  • (e) List of medical facilities locally available.

Copies of these station working orders shall be readily available with the Station Controller on duty. A copy shall be kept in a special marked binder in a conspicuous place in the station control room.

45. Prevention of Overcrowding

Rule 45
In case a service delay or other incident causes a build-up of passengers, the Station Controller shall decide when that build up is likely to be unmanageable.
Reduce Flow
Make a warning announcement and stop some/all inwards escalators. Switch out some/all inward automatic fare collection barriers. Instruct ticket sales staff to stop selling tickets.
Close Entrances
If overcrowding develops in the concourse despite above measures, passengers shall be advised to leave the station and the station entrances to be closed.
Restoration
When train services are restored, the restrictive measures may be progressively or completely removed depending upon the frequency of train services available.

46. Emergency Evacuation (Part 1)

Rule 46 (1-2)

The Station Controller shall control the evacuation from the control room or other location in the event of cessation of train services, risk of fire or any other emergency:

  • All automatic fare collection barriers shall be set to open freely in the exit direction and station staff deployed to assist.
  • Information and instruction shall be passed to the public by public address system and visual displays.
  • All inwards escalators may be stopped and used in the outwards direction.
  • All ticket sales shall be suspended and staff used to assist in passenger evacuation; all station exits shall be opened.

The station staff shall verify each area of the station that has been evacuated and thereafter when areas are verified as clear of passengers, the staff shall leave the station and secure it unless otherwise instructed by the Traffic Controller.

46 & 47. Evacuation (Fire) & Supervision

Rule 46 (3-6) & Rule 47

Fire Evacuation Protocols

  • General Fire: The Traffic Controller shall be informed so that he can instruct Train Operators not to stop the train at the station. Passengers instructed to use routes avoiding contaminated areas.
  • Concourse Fire: Train stopped for boarding only (quick evacuation). Train Operators announce to passengers not to alight.
  • Extensive Fire: Traffic Controller instructs approaching trains to stop at the previous station for evacuating passengers there.

47. Supervision of Train Movements

  • When station control is exercised from OCC, the Station Controller observes passage of trains on monitor and takes action if disrupted.
  • Any failure of any indication on panel or work station shall be reported immediately to the Traffic Controller.
  • Control of panel/workstation may only be taken with Traffic Controller's permission; operation of routes/points carried out per TC's instructions.

CHAPTER VII: Accident and Unusual Occurrences

Subject: Regulations on reporting accidents, duties in emergencies, stalled train assistance, evacuation procedures, flooding, and earthquake response.

48 & 49. Reporting & Duties in Emergency

Rules 48 & 49
48. Reporting Any accident or incident shall be reported by the authorised person concerned or any other person who notices it with utmost expediency to the Traffic Controller or the nearest Station Controller as soon as practicable.
49. Traffic Controller First ascertain the extent of injury... take prompt action to prevent further injuries... assess the potential effect on the train services. Keep a log of all reports and requests received.
49. Station Controller Take measures to prevent the situation becoming worse, render first aid if possible, arrange for the injured to be hospitalised. Evacuate the area concerned and take measures to prevent access... in extreme cases, the station may be closed.

Priority of Actions (Rule 49.7)

All staff of the metro railway shall deal with accidents and emergencies expeditiously with the following priorities, namely:

  1. Save life, prevent further injury and alleviate suffering;
  2. Protect the metro railway property and equipment;
  3. Take steps for preservation of clues;
  4. Inform the public of the effect on train services and the availability of alternative transport facilities;
  5. Restore the safe operation of the train services as quickly as practicable; and
  6. Restore normal services.

50. Train Stopped Between Stations

Rule 50 (1) - Assisting a Stalled Train
Phase Action Required
Initial Action In case the Train Operator cannot isolate a defect on his train and is unable to move it under its own power, he shall secure the train and request the Traffic Controller for assistance.
Approach Assisting train drives as close to the stalled train as possible under Coded Manual mode, then changes to RM mode or Cut out mode to proceed at reduced speed and stop about ten meters short of the stalled train.
Coupling Train Operator of defective train secures his train. Assisting train couples by mechanical means only and isolates all electrical connections to the defective train. Brakes of defective train are then released.
Movement The combined consist shall then be moved forward until the defective train has completely reached the platform and there after the passengers of the defective train shall be detrained at the station and the combined consist shall be moved to the depot.

50. Evacuation to a Train

Rule 50 (4-6)
Same Track Evacuation
Rule 50(4)		 Traffic on the adjacent track may be stopped. Assisting train moves slowly and stops ten meters short of the stalled train. Train Operators open appropriate doors/ramps, and supervise transfer. Once transferred, assisting train drives to station in RM mode or Cut out mode.
Adjacent Track Evacuation
Rule 50(5) (Except Twin Tunnel)		 Traffic Controller instructs assisting train to stop near stalled train in RM mode or Cut out mode. Two Train Operators supervise transfer of passengers via ramps or step ladders and the track. Particular care taken to inform passengers of the dangers of tripping on rails and other equipment.
Twin Tunnels Evacuation
Rule 50(6)		 Assisting train drives to a point near a cross passage as advised by the Traffic Controller. Operators open doors nearest to cross passage and supervise transfer via the cross passage and the track. Ensure all passengers are safely transferred and accounted for.

50. Evacuation on Foot & Traction Failure

Rule 50 (7-10)
Foot to Station
(Rule 50.7)		 Traffic Controller decides which station passengers are to be evacuated. Traffic suspended on adjacent track; third rail power switched off. Passengers detrained on track or raised walkway and directed to the station. Train Operator counts passengers leaving train, and Station Controller counts passengers arriving at platform to ensure all reached safely.
Viaduct/Shaft
(Rule 50.8)		 If train is near a suitable location, passengers evacuate by climbing down the via-duct or climbing up the evacuation shaft in tunnels. Station Controllers depute staff/security at safe point to assist. Passengers counted upon leaving stalled train and upon arrival at safe location.
Traction Failure
(Rule 50.9 & 10)		 If train cannot move due to traction failure, cut off traction power in affected section. Mandatory Requirement: In the case of third rail traction system, the third rail power supply shall be 'switched off' in all cases of evacuation in which passengers are required to come on the raised walkway or the track.

51. Train Divided

Rule 51
Irrevocable Emergency Brake: In case a train is stopped by application of an irrevocable emergency brake and cab signalling indications are normal, the Train Operator shall examine the TCMS panel... if indication of faults in multiple circuits affecting the whole train or rear cars of the train are present, the train shall not be moved until, it has been verified that the train is complete and coupled.
Train Parted The Train Operator shall first satisfy himself that no passenger has been injured or has fallen from the train.
Re-coupling Passengers shall be cleared of the open ends of the train and train re-coupled with the help of assisting staff as per special instructions, if possible in consultation with the Traffic Controller and rolling stock supervisor. Train withdrawn from passenger service at next station.
Cannot Re-couple In case the train cannot be re-coupled, the Train Operator shall inform the Traffic Controller and further action shall be taken as per special instructions.

52. Unusual Occurrences (Fire / Smoke)

Rule 52
Location Procedure
On Train (Between Stations) Train Operator shall inform the Traffic Controller, drive his train to the next station and detrain passengers. Traction power shall then be switched off, and in overhead traction territory, the pantographs of the affected train lowered, or in the third rail traction territory the current collection devices of the affected train retracted.
In Tunnel The Traffic Controller shall arrange with the auxiliary systems controller for the ventilation system to supply fresh air to the chosen route for evacuation before authorising detrainment of passengers.
On Station Station Controller verifies if alarm is genuine. If fire or smoke is present, arrange for passengers to be evacuated from the area concerned... if necessary, the station may be completely evacuated and the Traffic Controller may be requested to arrange for trains not to stop.
Adjacent Premises Report to Traffic Controller who informs chief controller and security controller. Maintain normal services unless or until a local inspection confirms that a potential danger exists.

53 & 54. Flooding & Other Unsafe Conditions

Rules 53 & 54
53. Flooding
  • Below rail fastenings: The Traffic Controller shall instruct the Train Operator to reduce the speed of their trains to twenty-five kilometre per hour when passing through the affected area.
  • Above rail fastenings: Passenger train service shall only be permitted under special instructions.
54. Look Out All authorised persons, and in particular the Train Operators and Station Controllers shall keep a look out for damaged or dislodged fixed equipment, broken or buckled rails, displaced overhead traction or third rail power conductors, construction activities/cranes, road accidents affecting viaducts, and any other obstruction on the track.
54. Earthquake The Traffic Controller shall instruct all trains to stop immediately and after the earthquake has subsided, the Traffic Controller may instruct each stranded Train Operator to proceed in RM mode or Cut out mode at walking speed after examining that the track is safe for train movement and free from obstruction to up the next station.

55. Accidents

Rule 55

Medical Aid & Emergency

  • In case of accidents, arrangements for medical aid, evacuation of sick, injured passengers, access for ambulance, staff and vehicles shall be made and included as per provisions specified in special instructions.
  • In the event of serious accident, the chief controller may, in consultation with senior management declare an emergency as per the provisions specified in special instructions.

Emergency Control

  • A senior member of the management shall be appointed as an emergency officer and shall set up an emergency control either at OCC or at the site depending on the nature of the occurrence.
  • The emergency officer shall be in overall charge of all the metro railway resources of staff and materials for the handling of the emergency and the coordination between the metro railway and external emergency agencies, such as fire, ambulance and police and utility services.

CHAPTER VIII: Systems of Working

Subject: Detailed rules governing the CATC system and various modes of train operation including Automatic, Manual, Restricted, and Cut-Out modes.

56. CATC System

Rule 56 (1-4)
Adoption The CATC system of working shall be adopted on the metro railway, for movement of trains between stations and between depot and the main line.
Working Principle The CATC system which includes CBTC works on the principle of target speed and target distance with Cab Signalling by means of continuous transmission between trackside and train through suitable approved means....
Movement Authority The limit of movement authority shall be the farthest point to which the train may safely proceed taking into account margins for error in speed and distance measurement, calculating braking distances and the equipment reaction times.
Max Safe Speed The maximum safe speed shall be the speed at which the train is permitted to travel without intervention by the train control and signalling system....

56. Modes of Train Operation

Rule 56 (5)

The CATC system shall provide the following modes of train operation, namely:

  • a UTO;
  • b DTO;
  • c AM, where provided;
  • d automatic reversal mode or automatic turn back mode, if provided;
  • e coded manual mode;
  • f RM Mode;
  • g Cut Out Mode; and
  • h ROS Mode, where provided.

57. Automatic Mode (AM)

Rule 57

In the AM, the train shall operate without intervention of the Train Operator except closing of train doors and starting from a station stop and it shall operate under the supervision and control of the ATP functions.

Movement Control Accelerate and decelerate the train by applying traction power, coasting and applying and releasing brakes; automatically control speed, acceleration, and stop the train at stations;
Safety Limits Determine continuously the maximum safe speed and limit of movement authority; prevent movement of the train in excess of the maximum safe speed and limit of movement authority;
Doors & Starting Prevent the train from starting if train doors or the Platform Screen Doors, where provided are not detected closed; train re-starting from a signal stop shall be automatic; and train starting or re-starting from a station stop shall be initiated by the Train Operator.

58. Automatic Reversal Mode

Rule 58

Function

This mode, where provided, is used to reverse the running direction of a train automatically in areas of the section specifically specified in the special instructions when train is at standstill.

Transition & Activation

The transition from AM or coded manual mode to automatic reversal mode is initiated automatically upon receiving the request for reversal operation from ATS and has to be acknowledged by the Train Operator by pressing the automatic reversal button at standstill.

The on-board ATP unit of leading cab activates the unit at trailing end on arrival at a station....

The train borne ATP unit shall return from automatic reversal mode to coded manual mode once the reversal operation has been carried out successfully and the Train Operator has unlocked the new leading cab for further running.

59. Coded Manual Mode

Rule 59

In coded manual mode the train shall be driven by the Train Operator, obeying cab signals.

System Limits Determine continuously the target speed and limit of movement authority; prevent train operation in excess of the target speed or limit of movement authority;
Warnings Provide audible and visual warning if the train speed exceeds the target speed or the maximum safe speed;
Doors Enable train doors and platform screen doors wherever provided, when the train is docked, enabling only the doors on the platform side of the train; and prevent the train from starting, if train doors or the Platform Screen Doors where provided are not detected closed.

60. RM Mode

Rule 60

The RM Mode is the default mode of operation and is automatically initiated, when the automatic train control train borne equipment is first powered and it remains in operation until sufficient conditions have been met to allow for a transfer to coded manual or AM.

The RM Mode shall be used in the absence of coded manual or AMs:

  • (a) To operate trains in depots;
  • (b) Following an emergency brake application on main line and absence of cab signals;
  • (c) For entry to and upto exit from the depot; and
  • (d) On instructions from the Traffic Controller.
Speed Limit In RM Mode, the train speed shall be limited to a maximum of twenty-five kilometre per hour enforced by on board ATP equipment.
Doors All Platform Screen Doors, where provided shall open automatically or have to be operated manually by the Train Operator or an authorised person from the local control panel.... The train doors on the correct side shall also have to be operated manually by the Train Operator.

61. Cut-out-Mode

Rule 61

The 'Cut Out' Mode, is intended for use in case of complete failure of train borne signalling and train control system.... Running of trains on mainline in Cut Out Mode is permitted only under instructions of the Traffic Controller.

Low Speed Cut-Out

...default mode of operation in automatic train control cut-out condition, in which the propulsion system would cut out traction of the train above twenty-five kilometre per hour speed and the Train Operator shall manually drive the train... and limit the speed of the train if it exceeds twenty-five kilometre per hour speed....

High Speed Cut-Out

...mode where provided, shall be employed only under specific instructions from the OCC, in which... the propulsion system would cut out traction of the train above forty kilometre per hour and the Train Operator shall manually drive the train... and limit the speed of the train if it exceeds forty kilometre per hour....

In cut-out-mode, the train shall be operated by the Train Operator in accordance with line side signals and on radio verbal instructions from the Traffic Controller. Both Platform Screen Doors and train doors on the correct side shall have to be operated manually by the Train Operator.

62. Run On Sight (ROS) Mode

Rule 62

In ROS Mode, which is a sub-mode to the Coded Manual Mode and only operates in the absence of ATP signals from train control ground equipments, the train is driven manually on line of sight and the speed is limited by ATP system to a maximum of twenty-five kilometre per hour.

Automatic Transition

When ATP signals from train control ground equipments are received, this mode automatically changes to Coded Manual Mode.

CHAPTER IX: Single Line Working

Subject: Guidelines for normal operations on single/twin single lines, and emergency single line working during track obstructions.

63. Adoption of Single Line Working

Rule 63

Single line working shall be adopted in the following situations, namely:

Normal Operation

Normal operation on a single line or twin single lines shall be adopted when the signalling system permits operation in both directions.

During Obstructions

When there is an obstruction on a portion of line between any two stations on one of the running lines, the train services may be continued in the affected section on the adjacent line in both directions using single line working on the unaffected track.

64. Implementation (Normal Operations)

Rule 64 (1)

Standard Protocol

The train shall operate under the authority of cab signals under Coded Manual Mode or AM (if signalling system permits) on the single line or on the respective line of twin single lines.

In Case of Signalling Failure:
In case of failure of cab signals under Coded Manual Mode or AM, the trains may be allowed to run under the RM Mode, ROS Mode or Cut Out Mode, under Special Instructions.

64. Implementation (During Obstruction)

Rule 64 (2)

Where a portion of a line is obstructed... the Traffic Controller may, after consultation with the chief controller decide to implement the single line working in the following manner:

Fleeting Single line working on the unaffected track shall be achieved by fleeting of trains in groups in one direction at intervals decided by the Traffic Controller followed by a group of trains in the other direction.
Stations The Traffic Controller shall before the train passes in the reverse direction, inform to the Station Controller at each of the stations. Station Controllers shall closely monitor crowding... and direct passengers on the correct platform by visual and audible announcement.
Train Control The Traffic Controller shall inform the Train Operators of all trains of the location and direction. Trains shall work under normal signalling including ATO in the normal direction of travel... in reverse direction of travel... train shall normally work under AM or Coded Manual Mode.

65. Signalling Failures

Rule 65

Suspension of Single Line Working

In the event of failure of a track side signalling equipment which makes RM operation necessary on all trains, single line working under sub-rule (2) of rule 64 shall be suspended immediately.

If necessary, train may still pass through the section in the normal direction of travel and after defect is repaired; single line working may be resumed.

72. Traction & Power Supply Control

Rule 72 (1-5)
Live System All traction and power distribution systems shall remain live at all times and shall only be switched off when necessary for maintenance of the equipment or protection of other maintenance activities.

Rule 72 (6)

Functionality

The emergency trip switches shall be used for switching off the traction power supply in a specified section in case of any emergency... keeping in view that approaching train may apply emergency brakes and still enter the affected area.

Locations & Operations

Rule 73

Security

Competency

Rule 87 (1)

The UTMS shall provide the following two modes of train operation, namely:

Unattended Train Operation (UTO)

Unattended Train Operation (UTO) Mode where Roving Attendant or ETO or Train Operator may or may not be on-board as per special instructions.

87. UTMS System Capabilities

Rule 87 (2)
Train Control Start, acceleration, deceleration and stoppage of train at stations... train doors and Platform Screen Doors... shall be opened and closed. Automatic routing of trains to and from stabling as per time table.

Rule 87 (3)

A Roving Attendant who is on-board a DTO or UTO train shall perform the following duties, namely:

  • Be present in the train and remain watchful;

  • Report any unusual occurrence to the Traffic Controller;
  • Take over control of train and perform duties of Train Operator when required in accordance with rule 32;
  • Do any other duty as assigned by general or special order.
OCC Monitoring: In DTO or UTO mode, the Traffic Controller shall use all information received at OCC to monitor and ensure smooth operation of trains... Whenever required, the Traffic Controller shall use remote commands to handle unusual occurrences.

Rules 91 & 92

91. Train Detained on Line

In case ATP system fails, irrevocable emergency brakes shall apply. Traffic Controller... shall attempt to release the brakes and move the train in UTO mode via remote commands. If it does not start, the Roving Attendant shall take over... and if still immovable, proceed in Cut-Out mode.

Traction & Axle Lock

  • In case traction power is lost on any motor car or bogie... the train need not be withdrawn from service and the OCC shall receive alarm of failure.
  • In the event of an axle lock, TCMS or TIMS, shall generate alarm(s) to OCC so as to ensure safe operation.

TCMS/TIMS & Brakes

  • On failure of a unit of sub-system... the train shall continue its journey upto the next station and failure shall result in suitable alarm for OCC.
  • Failure of brakes to apply or to release... shall be indicated as an alarm at OCC. In no case, the train should be moved without release of all the brakes.

101. Examination of Trains (Wake-Up Test)

Rule 101

UTMS shall perform wake-up test of each train before being offered for passenger service. Wake-up test examination shall ensure that the following functions of the train are working correctly and in particular safety devices:

  • (a) Cab signalling;
  • (b) Safety brake circuits including brake system;
  • (c) Train radio communication;
  • (d) CCTV of the train, CCTV communication link;
  • (e) Head and tail lights;
  • (f) Display panel of TCMS or TIMS;
  • (g) Horn;
  • (h) Miniature circuit breaker;
  • (i) Marker light, flasher light;
These tests shall result into a suitable log, clearly bringing out that the train is fit for passenger service and shall include the rake ID and results of this test.

103, 106 & 107. Doors, Stations & OCC Duties

Rules 103, 106 & 107
103. Auto Docking In UTO mode when the train is docked properly and permitted by the ATP door release, the train doors shall open automatically... the Platform Screen Doors... shall open automatically on the correct side.
106. Station Fire In case the fire is extensive, the trains should not approach the station, and the Station Controller shall inform the Traffic Controller to take steps to prevent the trains coming towards the affected station, to stop or hold at the previous stations.
107. OCC Passenger Alarms In case a passenger emergency alarm is operated in the train, the nominated staff in the OCC shall use video communication and try to establish the reason... and unless there is a clear and immediate danger... allow the train to continue to the next station.

108 & 109. Stalled Trains & Derailments

Rules 108 & 109

108. Derailment & Evacuation

  • In case a train cannot be moved as a result of derailment or axle lock... the train shall switch-on the flasher light and send alarm to the OCC and Traffic Controller to stop all the trains in the affected area.
  • If no Roving Attendant on board, Traffic Controller shall release electrical hold on evacuation door... and ask passengers to self-evacuate, in case of time critical emergencies (e.g. fire).

109. Train Divided

  • In case, the train is divided, irrevocable emergency brakes shall be applied and TCMS or TIMS shall raise an alarm.
  • If train is parted, Traffic Controller shall first satisfy himself that no passenger... has been injured... announcements shall be done to guide passengers for self-evacuation.

111, 112 & 117. Environment & Special Operations

Rules 111, 112 & Chapter XIII
111. Flooding The Traffic Controller shall apply TSR of twenty-five kilometre per hour on the affected area, if the water level is below the level of rail fastenings. If above fastenings, passenger train service permitted only under special instructions.
112. Earthquake In the event of an earthquake, the Traffic Controller shall stop all the trains immediately by relevant command(s) from OCC and also by instructing Roving Attendant(s).
117. Initial Stages CHAPTER XIII: Special procedures framed under approved special instructions shall apply to the initial stage of operation... when any section of the metro railway shall be opened without ATP or when signalling and train control is not available from the OCC.
